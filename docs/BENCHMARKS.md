# Benchmarks

This document states the bar Greenmantle is measured against, target by target, with the procedure for producing each number. It is the canonical reference for the production run: when a benchmark here and a claim elsewhere in `docs/` disagree, this file wins and the other file gets fixed.

## Craft, not identity

The games named throughout — StarCraft II above all, plus Brood War, Warcraft III, Command & Conquer, Age of Empires II, Company of Heroes, Supreme Commander — are the bar for **craft**. They are not the bar for **identity**.

Craft is the part that transfers: every accepted order produces a confirmation, and the confirmation lands where the order was accepted. A unit is identifiable from its black fill. A disabled button prints the unmet requirement rather than only greying. Fog remembers the building you scouted. Income per base is hard-capped so taking ground you cannot fully defend is compulsory rather than optional. A death animation finishes before the geometry leaves the scene. None of that is StarCraft's — it is what a competently made real-time strategy game owes its player, and StarCraft happens to be where most of it was solved best.

Identity is the part that does not transfer: ten control groups, three hotkey profiles, a 12-unit selection cap, glowing blue-cyan crystal, per-mission unit unlocks, medal-chasing practice drills, APM in the post-match screen, a commands-per-minute tempo signature. Those exist because StarCraft is a game about executing fast. Greenmantle is a game about **commanding intent**: the player is an Operations Commander issuing objectives and doctrine to an army that executes intelligently, and direct per-unit control remains available forever as the deliberately *worse* way to play (D-003; `GAME_DESIGN.md` §§1–2). Importing an execution affordance because a great game has one is how a project accidentally rebuilds the thing it exists to replace.

The line is load-bearing enough that every comparison below states which side of it the comparison sits on, and the "Do not copy" section near the end names, for each rejected import, the Greenmantle decision it would violate. Where a benchmark's threshold is a design judgement rather than a measured or published figure, the text says so. Where the underlying research was labelled "measured" but nothing was measured, the label has been downgraded and the reason recorded.

Greenmantle intends to beat these games on feel, clarity, rigour and polish. That is not a slogan: on several targets here Greenmantle can produce *stronger* evidence than StarCraft can, because combat consumes zero RNG (D-019) and the whole simulation is deterministic plain data (D-001, D-010). Where SC2 needs a decade of ladder statistics to argue that a matchup is fair, Greenmantle can prove spawn-side neutrality on a single seed. That advantage is real, it is already paid for, and it is currently unused.

## The browser constraint, stated honestly

Greenmantle runs in a browser: 1080p, 30 fps, 100+ units, on roughly 2017 integrated graphics, **from a cold page load** (D-006). `GAME_DESIGN.md` §10 makes "playable directly from the game's website" a platform requirement rather than a nicety. That constraint is real and it forecloses specific things.

**What it genuinely forecloses, and the substitute:**

- **A warmed pipeline cache.** A native RTS ships compiled shaders; a browser recompiles every program on every cold visit, and a lazily-compiled material stalls the first frame that needs it. Substitute: compile every painterly `ShaderMaterial` during load and assert `sharedMaterialCount()` reaches its full expected value before the first `renderer.render()` call. Note that `PerformanceMonitor.sample()` discards any frame with `frameMs >= 250`, so a 300 ms first-use compile is invisible to every percentile the harness reports — a polish layer that compiles on demand can pass the frame benchmark and still visibly stutter.
- **A multi-gigabyte local asset library.** SC2 organises sound into roughly 16 channel groups because it is managing tens of thousands of streamed assets; it also ships four to five damage-typed death variants per unit. Neither is available here. Substitute: synthesise the high-frequency audio layer (footfall, impact, UI tick, command acknowledgement) in Web Audio so it adds **0 KB to the critical path**, and take one death presentation whose variation is derived from `unit.id` and the death tick rather than from a library or from `world.rngState`.
- **A submission ceiling materially below native.** Browsers validate every GL command above the driver. This is the one place the constraint changes architecture rather than content: per-unit `THREE.Group`s are not a slow version of the right answer, they are the wrong answer. Substitute: instanced pools keyed to the existing LOD bands.
- **A raw input path.** `getCoalescedEvents` appears nowhere in `src/` and would recover samples, not time. There is no substitute; the honest response is to stop spending latency elsewhere — acknowledge on the earliest input event that determines the order rather than on mouseup.
- **WebAudio unlock plus roughly 20–50 ms of output latency** once audio lands. No substitute; budget for it rather than pretending impact sync is free.

**What the constraint does not excuse.** Roughly 700 unit-attributable draw calls at the 200-unit preset; per-unit geometry allocation with no disposal on death; a transparent ring that is never made invisible and is submitted at every LOD including "world"; a nested `world.units` scan inside the per-frame view loop; two hotkeys that issue a command and pan the camera simultaneously; six player-issuable commands that mutate the world and cannot report a reason; cyan order markers; a debug telemetry panel on a first-time player's screen. None of those are browser costs. They are craft debt, and citing D-006 for any of them is dishonest.

**One correction worth stating plainly, because it appears in older analysis:** `src/core/loop.ts` drives an uncapped `requestAnimationFrame`. Only the fixed 30 Hz simulation is quantised, and the render callback receives the leftover alpha so views interpolate at any framerate. There is no 30 fps render cap in the codebase to remove.

**And one honest ceiling on ambition:** `SUPPLY_MAX` is 200 per team and every combat unit costs 2 supply, so a maxed team fields roughly 92–100 combat units and a 1v1 field tops out near 200. 400 units is not "out of scope by preference" — it is unreachable without editing `src/data/tuning.ts`. No SC2-parity claim about unit counts is available to make, and none is made below.

## How to read the tables

Each dimension gets one table: **Benchmark | Reference | Target | How we verify**. Targets are countable. Where a "today" figure appears it was produced against this repository and is labelled with how: *measured* means someone ran it, *documented* means it was read directly from source, *derived* means it is closed-form arithmetic over source constants, *estimated* means it is a design judgement or a recollection. Prose under each table carries the reasoning, the corrections, and the confidence caveats.

## 1. Control responsiveness and command feel

This is the dimension where the player's hands touch the game, and it is the one where Brood War's example is most instructive: it carries roughly 125 ms of real command delay at TR24/Low latency and still reads as responsive, because the *local confirmation* is not delayed. Immediate acknowledgement matters more than tick rate (D-004), and it is what will hide the 66–133 ms of lockstep input delay that D-018 commits to when multiplayer arrives.

**Notes.** Partial acknowledgement coverage is worse than none: the player learns that a marker means "received" and reads its absence as rejection. The silent path that matters most is `addChainStep`, because chain steps are the doctrine layer `UI_BLUEPRINT.md` calls the primary gameplay layer. A marker drawn where the units will not go is worse still — it confirms something that did not happen.

Target 1.2 is the single most damaging defect in the dimension: `a` arms attack-move and pans the camera left; `s` issues Stop and pans backward. `src/input/keyboard.ts:20` sets `state.keys[k] = true` before calling `onKey`, so `preventDefault()` in `main.ts` does not stop the pan, and the pan is velocity-integrated every frame at roughly 27 world units per second at the default distance. Arming attack-move slides the battlefield out from under the click the player is about to make. Rebindability is not the benchmark; one table both lists read from is what makes zero collisions durable rather than a one-time fix — and a hardcoded WASD-plus-letters layout is a floor problem for non-QWERTY and one-handed players regardless.

Target 1.4 is upstream of everything else here. `Ctrl+N` forms a squad from the current selection, so the entire D-003 intent hierarchy is entered through the box-select gesture. It fails worse here than in SC2 because this camera can get much closer: at distance 2.5 with a 45-degree vertical FOV the viewport is roughly 2 world units tall, so a 1 m anchor offset is on the order of half the screen. Confidence on 1.4 is **estimated** — the mechanism and the offset are read from source and the geometry is arithmetic, but the zoom at which it begins failing has not been measured in a browser, and the half-silhouette threshold is a chosen number.

Target 1.5 carries an unresolved design decision rather than hiding it. `followSelection()` (`src/input/mouse.ts:39-46`) sets `ui.selectedSquadId` only when every selected unit belongs to one squad, so "shift-right-click appends to the squad's chain" would silently no-op for an ad-hoc box selection — reproducing exactly the silent-rejection failure 1.6 exists to eliminate. Whether the gesture implicitly forms a squad or refuses with a stated reason must be decided; the benchmark is not met until it is. `AUTOMATION.maxChainSteps` is 6, so the gesture must also refuse the seventh step visibly rather than swallowing it.

Two measurement caveats. First, a "milliseconds from pointer handler to composited frame" latency target was cut: on the eight paths that already have markers it is met by construction, and on the paths with no marker it is undefined — a benchmark that cannot fail is not a benchmark. What survives are the falsifiable parts, 1.1(c) and 1.1(d). Second, a click-to-sim-apply budget with lockstep headroom was cut outright: `grep -rin lockstep src/` returns four doc comments and zero implementation, so a 133 ms budget can be asserted but not measured.

Finally, a record correction: `docs/TODO.md` line 65 marks "Immediate input acknowledgement (marker, cursor state, sound)" as complete. Cursor state and sound do not exist anywhere in `src/`. The project believes this box is ticked.

One target was cut wholesale: a minimap area/footprint/alert benchmark. D-014 consequence 5 also leaves the minimap an explicitly open question, so setting an area floor would pre-empt a decision the project has deliberately not made.

## 4. Strategic depth, decision density and matchup integrity

This is where Greenmantle's determinism becomes a craft *advantage* rather than a constraint. SC2 needed a decade of ladder statistics to argue that its matchups are fair because its outcomes come from human execution and its simulation rolls dice. **Notes.** 6.6 drops the "worst frame ≤50 ms" pass/fail that earlier analysis proposed, because `docs/PERFORMANCE_TESTING.md` itself states that worst frame time "is diagnostic only and can be distorted by browser or OS interruptions" — gating on it contradicts the project's own measurement discipline. Use p99.

6.3 is the target worth pulling forward. Without it, hit feedback, death feedback and every combat sound are not merely unbuilt, they are *unbuildable*: a renderer polling `World` cannot observe a transient, because `Unit` has no `prevHp` and `attackCd` resetting is the only hit tell, which aliases at 30 Hz sim against 60 Hz render. It also absorbs the only durable requirement from a removed impact-sync target: the event carries the tick damage applied on, and presentation schedules from that tick rather than from the frame the renderer noticed it. The hazard to guard: `hash()` in `snapshot.ts` enumerates fields explicitly, so a new array is silently excluded — which is the point, and also exactly the failure D-010 describes. Make the array write-only from the sim's side, clear it at the top of `simStep` so `restore()` cannot resurrect a stale one, and assert hash identity with and without it. Confidence on 6.3 is **estimated** — the current-state grep is verified, but the design is unbuilt and its cost unmeasured. Confidence on 6.4's numbers is likewise **estimated**: the zero-state is verified, every threshold is proposed and none has been measured in a browser.

6.1's scope is deliberately narrow, and the reason is a sequencing constraint rather than a technical one. D-014's own sequencing paragraph says the war table should land **before** the remaining art work on units, buildings and scenery, so that work is done once against the real camera. This target is therefore scoped to what is camera-independent: local transforms on primitives that already exist in `makeUnitView()`. A walk cycle on a part's local matrix reads identically from any angle at any scale and needs no re-authoring. Impostors, close-LOD detail meshes and per-tier animation budgets are explicitly excluded and stay behind D-014.

6.2's backward-seek clause is not padding: a render-side corpse cache is derived state that a replay scrub can desynchronise from the sim, and that bug is invisible until someone scrubs.

6.5 leads with the mission inspector because it is the one thing `UI_BLUEPRINT.md`'s "Spectator Integration" section claims as this design's advantage over a StarCraft broadcast — an observer who can read *why* a squad is where it is sees something SC2 has no state to show. That advantage is worth exactly nothing while the entire replay layer lives behind a dev query parameter. D-012 makes replays a product requirement; D-027 already landed `Mission` as real hashed sim state, so the inspector has a primitive to read rather than a fiction to invent.

Two proposed metrics were excised as unmeasurable. A "5 named animation clips × 100% of types" coverage count is passed by a stub that exposes five names and moves nothing — it measures a naming convention. And "at least 12 of ~20 WCS Observer capabilities" has no definition of one capability, so any two reviewers produce different counts from the same build.

## Where we are furthest from the bar

Ranked worst first, grounded in `docs/CURRENT_STATE.md`, `docs/TODO.md`, `docs/BUGS.md` and the source under `src/`. "Worst" weighs both the size of the gap and how much else depends on it. Figures carry the same confidence labels used above.

1. **Side-neutrality (4.1) — measured double failure.** Mean height asymmetry at the base anchors is 0.91 over 200 seeds, exceeding the 0.60 high-ground threshold in 159 of 200 seeds, and an identical mirrored 10v10 resolves 10–0 for whichever team was inserted into `world.units` first. This is first because it invalidates every other combat measurement taken on top of it, and because it breaks the only balance claim `GAME_DESIGN.md` states as an absolute. Neither failure is caught by any test; `tests/world.test.ts` and `tests/mapBoundary.test.ts` assert position symmetry only.
2. **No advance to contact (4.5) — measured.** `src/sim/movement.ts` is 33 lines and `u.target` is the only thing that moves a unit; `stepCombat` writes `targetId` that nothing acts on. Two idle 10-unit lines placed 3.0 apart fought for 120 simulated seconds and neither took a point of damage. Every fight in the game today happens only where the player has parked units within 1.74 of each other. `BEHAVIOURS` is `{move, attackmove, gather, patrol}` — confirmed — so the withdraw-at-threshold behaviour `UI_BLUEPRINT.md` already writes templates against cannot be expressed.
3. **The economy gives no reason to leave home, and dies in under four minutes (4.3) — measured.** 800 of 800 nodes across 200 seeds sit inside the starting control radii; there is no marginal-return knee at any worker count up to 14; the rival AI strips one side's entire share in 1.7–2.2 minutes. The knock-on is what puts this third: peak simultaneous unit count in a real match is roughly 32 per side, so **D-006's 100+ unit target cannot be exercised by any actual match on this map**, and every benchmark that assumes large armies is untestable until this is resized.
4. **Onboarding does not exist as a first-time surface (3.1, 3.2, 3.3) — documented.** `enableAi` is unconditional at `main.ts:49` and `createAi` sets `nextThinkTick: 0`, so the rival acts on tick 0 with zero gated beats. The default page ships 22-plus live keystrokes, 122 words across 8 always-on panels, a `#debug` telemetry readout and a `T` throttle key. The guided sequence covers 0 of the 5 systems the game is actually about. `STORAGE_KEY 'longbarrow:tutorial-complete'` is written twice and read nowhere, so a first visit is indistinguishable from a hundredth.
5. **Nothing about damage state reaches the screen (2.2) — documented.** `grep -rniE "health|\bhp\b" src/render src/ui` returns zero hits, confirmed this session. A player watching a fight cannot tell which side is winning except by counting corpses afterwards.
6. **Intel is not simulation state, and the AI is omniscient (4.4) — documented.** `src/sim/ai.ts`'s own header says it reads only what it could legitimately see and admits this "is currently on trust"; `pickObjective()` scans every building regardless of team or visibility and `expansionSpot()` reads every node on the map. Meanwhile `src/ui/fogOfWar.ts` is presentation-only by design. §3's promise that "full scouting should always be possible for a player willing to invest in it" is currently vacuous — scouting costs nothing and reveals nothing the AI did not already know. The fix is gated on rewriting `FogOfWarField` as plain arrays (D-010) and paying for it in `hash()` (D-012).
7. **The whole animation, death and event layer is at zero (6.1, 6.2, 6.3) — documented.** No child transform under `detailRoot` is ever touched after construction; `stepReaper()` runs in the same `simStep` as `stepCombat()` and the view is deleted on the next render pass, so units vanish between two frames; and `grep -rn "event" src/sim/` returns nothing, confirmed. The event channel ranks here rather than lower because it is the one item that gets structurally expensive to retrofit once the renderer's polling shape is settled.
8. **No audio subsystem at all (6.4) — documented.** `grep -rniE "audio|sound|AudioContext|\.ogg|\.wav|music"` across `src/` returns zero hits, confirmed, and there is no `src/audio/`. This is scheduled to Phase 4 and is correctly not being worked on now — but `docs/TODO.md` line 65 marks "Immediate input acknowledgement (marker, cursor state, sound)" as **complete**, and two of those three channels have no implementation.
9. **Unit-attributable draw calls and unbounded GPU object growth (5.2, 5.4) — estimated for the count, documented for the mechanism.** Per-unit `THREE.Group`s give roughly 700 unit draw calls at the 200-unit preset; every unit allocates fresh geometry and 3–4 non-shared materials; the prune path calls neither `geometry.dispose()` nor `material.dispose()`, so every dead unit leaks for the life of the page. Two of these are one-line fixes: the Legionnaire `wall` ring is never made invisible and is parented outside `detailRoot`, so 100 invisible transparent draws are submitted at every distance including "world". B-002 has recorded "100-unit performance is unverified" as open since the file was created, and this is the reason to expect it to fail when measured.
10. **No observer surface outside `?dev` (6.5) — documented.** 0 of 4. `src/replay/timeline.ts` is imported only by the dev sandbox and `src/replay/director.ts` is imported by nothing at all. The mission inspector — the one thing this design can show that a StarCraft broadcast cannot — has real hashed state behind it under D-027 and no surface in front of it.
11. **Hotkey collisions and camera runaway (1.2, 1.3) — documented.** `a` and `s` each issue a command and pan the camera, and `keyboard.ts:20` sets the key state before `onKey` runs so `preventDefault()` does not stop the pan; arming attack-move slides the battlefield out from under the click the player is about to make. Separately, `src/input/keyboard.ts` has no blur, visibilitychange or pointerleave handler, so a key held through Alt-Tab pans forever.
12. **Acknowledgement coverage and marker truthfulness (1.1) — documented.** 7 of 10 destination-bearing sites draw a marker; `place`, `addChainStep` and `assignBuilders`-on-success are silent, and `addChainStep` is the doctrine layer. Near the map rim the marker is drawn at the raw raycast hit while `cmdMove` clamps the destination inward by unit radius, so the confirmation states something the simulation did not do. Six of the highest-frequency commands return `void` and cannot report a reason at all.
13. **Overlay chrome exceeds the project's own budget (2.5) — measured, 17.57% against a stated ≤15%.** Two panels account for the entire overage: a permanent 14-line control-scheme card and a developer readout, both shipping on the live page rather than behind `?dev`. Removing them lands the page at 10.05%.

Two cross-cutting blockers should be cleared before much of this can be measured at all. **No performance baseline is committed anywhere in the repo** (6.6 step 0), so every "≤15% over baseline" clause in dimension 6 and the before/after half of dimension 5 are currently unverifiable. And **the only path to replay export is the `?dev` panel**, which also exposes a tick counter, pause, speed control, omniscient vision and a scrubber — so the instrument for 3.6 contaminates the measurement it is meant to take.

Finally, three records in `docs/` are out of date and will mislead anyone using them to prioritise against this document. `docs/CURRENT_STATE.md` lists "Research UI" as the single biggest gap and `docs/TODO.md` leaves it unchecked, but `src/ui/researchPanel.ts` exists and is wired at `main.ts:80`; the real gap is that the tutorial never mentions research. `docs/TODO.md` line 65 marks immediate input acknowledgement complete when two of its three named channels do not exist. And D-004's prose annotates that item as "not yet built", which was true when written and is no longer. Fix the records rather than the benchmarks.
